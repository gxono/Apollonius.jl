Unbounded Regions: Half-Planes, Strips & Angles

Not every region in the package is bounded. APHalfPlane2, APStrip2 and APAngle2 are the three unbounded members of the APSet family, genuine regions of the plane (they support in, distance, and the usual transforms), but with infinite area, so none of them is an APRegion/APPolygon and none has an area/perimeter. APAngle2 already has its own worked-example section; see Angles on the Points, Lines & Rays page. This page covers the other two.

TypeRepresentsBounded by
APHalfPlane2everything on one side of a line (that line included)one APLine
APStrip2the band between two parallel lines (both included)two parallel APLines
APAngle2the infinite wedge between two rays from a shared vertextwo APRays; see Angles

Being unbounded, none of the three has a finite extent to report: APBoundingBox returns the empty box for all three (see Bounding boxes for what that means and why), they still get translate/rotate/homothety/reflectioned normally, they just don't contribute anything if mixed into a @boundingbox/ @to_luxor_picture block alongside bounded shapes.

APHalfPlane2

A half-plane needs a boundary line and a choice of which side is "inside". Two equivalent ways to say that:

using Apollonius

l = APLine(APPoint(0.0, 0.0), APPoint(0.0, 4.0))   # the y-axis

hp1 = APHalfPlane2(l, -1)                   # side = -1: right of p1->p2, see side_of_line
hp2 = APHalfPlane2(l, APPoint(1.0, 0.0))    # "whichever side this point is on", also -1
hp1 == hp2
true

side = +1/-1 follows side_of_line's own convention (left/right of the line oriented p1 -> p2); the point-based form is usually easier to reason about, since you rarely think in terms of left/right directly. Passing a point on the boundary is an error: there's no side to pick:

try
    APHalfPlane2(l, APPoint(0.0, 2.0))    # on l itself
catch e
    e
end
ArgumentError("APHalfPlane2: interior_point must not lie on boundary")

p in hp is a closed test (the boundary line itself counts as inside):

APPoint(1.0, 0.0) in hp1, APPoint(0.0, 0.0) in hp1, APPoint(-1.0, 0.0) in hp1
(true, true, false)

distance(p, hp) is 0.0 from anywhere inside, and the perpendicular distance to the boundary from outside; pass mode=:boundary to always get the distance to the boundary line, even from inside:

distance(APPoint(1.0, 0.0), hp1), distance(APPoint(-3.0, 0.0), hp1)   # (0.0, 3.0): the second point is outside
(0.0, 3.0)
distance(APPoint(1.0, 0.0), hp1; mode=:boundary)   # 1.0, even though the point is already inside
1.0

rotate/homothety/translate move the boundary and leave side unchanged: a rotation or a homothety of any ratio is always orientation-preserving in 2D, so "the same physical side" is still the same side value afterward. reflection about a point is the same story, but reflecting about a line is a true mirror: it reverses orientation, so side flips too, otherwise the reflected half-plane would describe the wrong side of its own (also reflected) boundary:

reflection(hp1, APPoint(1.0, 1.0)).side,                                       # point reflection: side unchanged (-1)
    reflection(hp1, APLine(APPoint(0.0, 0.0), APPoint(1.0, 1.0))).side          # line reflection: side flips (+1)
(-1, 1)

From a point and a normal

APHalfPlane2(p, normal) builds the half-plane whose boundary passes through p perpendicular to normal, on the side that normal points to:

hp_n = APHalfPlane2(APPoint(0.0, 1.0), APVector(0.0, 1.0))
APPoint(0.0, 2.0) in hp_n, APPoint(0.0, 0.0) in hp_n
(true, false)

APStrip2

The closed band between two parallel lines: think of it as an APHalfPlane2 closed off on both sides instead of just one:

l1 = APLine(APPoint(0.0, 0.0), APPoint(1.0, 0.0))
l2 = APLine(APPoint(0.0, 3.0), APPoint(1.0, 3.0))
s = APStrip2(l1, l2)

strip_width(s)   # 3.0: the perpendicular distance between the two lines
3.0

The constructor checks parallelism itself and throws if the two lines aren't parallel: there's no sensible strip otherwise:

try
    APStrip2(l1, APLine(APPoint(0.0, 0.0), APPoint(1.0, 1.0)))
catch e
    e
end
ArgumentError("APStrip2: line1 and line2 must be parallel")

in, distance and the transforms all work exactly like APHalfPlane2, except a strip has no side bookkeeping to worry about: "the band between two lines" doesn't depend on either line's own orientation, so membership is simply recomputed fresh after any transform:

APPoint(0.5, 1.5) in s, APPoint(0.5, 5.0) in s
(true, false)
distance(APPoint(0.5, 5.0), s), distance(APPoint(0.5, 5.0), s; mode=:boundary)
(2.0, 2.0)
s_rotated = rotate(s, pi / 4)
strip_width(s_rotated) ≈ strip_width(s)   # rotation preserves the perpendicular distance
true

See Drawing with Luxor.jl for how APHalfPlane2/APStrip2 render (as their boundary line(s), since the region itself is unbounded) and Affine Maps for how a general APAffineMap applies to both.

From a line and a width

APStrip2(l, width) is the strip between l and the parallel line at distance width on its left (on its right for a negative width), built with offset_line:

st_w = APStrip2(APLine(APPoint(0.0, 0.0), APPoint(1.0, 0.0)), 2.0)
APPoint(0.0, 1.0) in st_w, APPoint(0.0, 3.0) in st_w, strip_width(st_w)
(true, false, 2.0)
