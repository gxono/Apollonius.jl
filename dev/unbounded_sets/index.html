Unbounded Regions: Half-Planes, Strips & Angles

Not every region in the package is bounded. EGHalfPlane2, EGStrip2 and EGAngle2 are the three unbounded members of the EGSet family — genuine regions of the plane (they support in, distance, and the usual transforms), but with infinite area, so none of them is an EGRegion/EGPolygon and none has an area/perimeter. EGAngle2 already has its own worked-example section — see Angles on the Points, Lines & Rays page. This page covers the other two.

TypeRepresentsBounded by
EGHalfPlane2everything on one side of a line (that line included)one EGLine
EGStrip2the band between two parallel lines (both included)two parallel EGLines
EGAngle2the infinite wedge between two rays from a shared vertextwo EGRays — see Angles

Being unbounded, none of the three has a finite extent to report: EGBoundingBox returns the empty box for all three (see Bounding boxes for what that means and why) — they still get translate/rotate/homothety/reflectioned normally, they just don't contribute anything if mixed into a @boundingbox/ @to_luxor_picture block alongside bounded shapes.

EGHalfPlane2

A half-plane needs a boundary line and a choice of which side is "inside". Two equivalent ways to say that:

using EuclideanGeometry

l = EGLine(EGPoint(0.0, 0.0), EGPoint(0.0, 4.0))   # the y-axis

hp1 = EGHalfPlane2(l, -1)                   # side = -1: right of p1->p2, see side_of_line
hp2 = EGHalfPlane2(l, EGPoint(1.0, 0.0))    # "whichever side this point is on" — also -1
hp1 == hp2
true

side = +1/-1 follows side_of_line's own convention (left/right of the line oriented p1 -> p2); the point-based form is usually easier to reason about, since you rarely think in terms of left/right directly. Passing a point on the boundary is an error — there's no side to pick:

try
    EGHalfPlane2(l, EGPoint(0.0, 2.0))    # on l itself
catch e
    e
end
ArgumentError("EGHalfPlane2: interior_point must not lie on boundary")

p in hp is a closed test (the boundary line itself counts as inside):

EGPoint(1.0, 0.0) in hp1, EGPoint(0.0, 0.0) in hp1, EGPoint(-1.0, 0.0) in hp1
(true, true, false)

distance(p, hp) is 0.0 from anywhere inside, and the perpendicular distance to the boundary from outside; pass mode=:boundary to always get the distance to the boundary line, even from inside:

distance(EGPoint(1.0, 0.0), hp1), distance(EGPoint(-3.0, 0.0), hp1)   # (0.0, 3.0): the second point is outside
(0.0, 3.0)
distance(EGPoint(1.0, 0.0), hp1; mode=:boundary)   # 1.0, even though the point is already inside
1.0

rotate/homothety/translate move the boundary and leave side unchanged — a rotation or a homothety of any ratio is always orientation-preserving in 2D, so "the same physical side" is still the same side value afterward. reflection about a point is the same story, but reflecting about a line is a true mirror: it reverses orientation, so side flips too, otherwise the reflected half-plane would describe the wrong side of its own (also reflected) boundary:

reflection(hp1, EGPoint(1.0, 1.0)).side,                                       # point reflection: side unchanged (-1)
    reflection(hp1, EGLine(EGPoint(0.0, 0.0), EGPoint(1.0, 1.0))).side          # line reflection: side flips (+1)
(-1, 1)

EGStrip2

The closed band between two parallel lines — think of it as an EGHalfPlane2 closed off on both sides instead of just one:

l1 = EGLine(EGPoint(0.0, 0.0), EGPoint(1.0, 0.0))
l2 = EGLine(EGPoint(0.0, 3.0), EGPoint(1.0, 3.0))
s = EGStrip2(l1, l2)

strip_width(s)   # 3.0: the perpendicular distance between the two lines
3.0

The constructor checks parallelism itself and throws if the two lines aren't parallel — there's no sensible strip otherwise:

try
    EGStrip2(l1, EGLine(EGPoint(0.0, 0.0), EGPoint(1.0, 1.0)))
catch e
    e
end
ArgumentError("EGStrip2: line1 and line2 must be parallel")

in, distance and the transforms all work exactly like EGHalfPlane2, except a strip has no side bookkeeping to worry about — "the band between two lines" doesn't depend on either line's own orientation, so membership is simply recomputed fresh after any transform:

EGPoint(0.5, 1.5) in s, EGPoint(0.5, 5.0) in s
(true, false)
distance(EGPoint(0.5, 5.0), s), distance(EGPoint(0.5, 5.0), s; mode=:boundary)
(2.0, 2.0)
s_rotated = rotate(s, pi / 4)
strip_width(s_rotated) ≈ strip_width(s)   # rotation preserves the perpendicular distance
true

See Drawing with Luxor.jl for how EGHalfPlane2/EGStrip2 render (as their boundary line(s), since the region itself is unbounded) and Affine Maps for how a general EGAffineMap applies to both.
